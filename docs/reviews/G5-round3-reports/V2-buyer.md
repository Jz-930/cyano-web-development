# G5 Round 3 — Buyer review (COO, ~800-person NA distribution & professional services company)

Reviewer persona: COO who owns AI rollout in operations, cares about ROI and vendor maturity, has seen a dozen AI vendor sites this quarter. English edition only, judged from screenshots.

## Five-second answer (en-home-d-01 only)

- **What they do:** Build custom AI tools: chat assistants over company knowledge, proposal drafting, data queries. They also run some of that work for you as a managed service.
- **For whom:** "Enterprise", but it's vague. The headline says "your everyday work" and the demo is an IT helpdesk bot, so I can't tell whether they target ops, sales or IT, or what size of company.
- **Confidence:** About 70% on what they do, about 30% on who it's for. The 80% / <3s numbers registered. The chat mockup with a cut-off message ("doesn't connect. What should I") and a button sitting on top of it registered too.

## Scores (1–7)

**R-TRUST**

1. **Maturity / reliability: 3.** The site looks like a well-funded firm. The content reads like four unnamed people with six projects, no address and an `.app` email. The design writes checks the substance doesn't cash.
2. **Willing to hand them an important AI project: 2.** Maybe a small paid pilot after a reference call. I would not give them anything that touches customer data or ERP until I know who they are, where they're incorporated and where my data lives.
3. **Believe they delivered real enterprise projects: 3.** Some of it is probably real. But the "client" screenshots are branded CYANO TECH, are in Chinese, use "Jane Doe" placeholders, and one dashboard labels the headline 80% as a *goal*. Nothing is attributable.
4. **Website professionalism overall: 5.** Clean, consistent and grown-up visually. It loses points for an overlap bug on mobile, a clipped "Click to enlarge" pill, a near-invisible headline, the same diagram reused everywhere, and Chinese-language UI on an English site.

**Other**

5. **First-impression polish: 5.** The dark hero, big type and live-looking product panel feel modern. The cropped chat bubble, with a button sitting on it, keeps it from being a 6.
6. **Effort to understand: 5 (fairly easy).** Four solution areas and three engagement models are clear by the second screen. What's hard is figuring out who they are, not what they sell.
7. **Two case studies: real delivered projects? Unsure, leaning no as presented.**
   - Proposal case: the "client system" is branded "CYANO TECH InvestiGenie" and written entirely in Chinese. It looks like their own product demo, not a client deployment.
   - Wiki case: the dashboard shows "Jane Doe / IT Operations", "INC-10458" and "80% Ticket Deflection **Goal**". It looks like a Figma mockup built to match the marketing numbers.
   - Neither case has a client role, a quote, a volume baseline, a dollar figure or a measurement method. Each has a timeline (10 weeks; 4-week pilot + 8-week rollout), which helps a little.
8. **Look and feel: premium-leaning but generic.** It's deliberate: a consistent teal/charcoal palette, good spacing, restrained motion cues. But it's the 2024–26 "AI startup template" look: dark hero, eyebrow labels with a square bullet, giant light-weight headlines, and one node-graph doodle reused on every page header and every insight thumbnail. Sketch avatars instead of people are the single cheapest-looking choice. Nothing here is distinctively Cyano.
9. **Memorability:** "From two days to 15 minutes" for proposals. A distant second is the latency slider on the Technology page (181 ms vs 1,615 ms), which I'd also remember as "illustrative data, not real". I wouldn't remember the name Cyano or any person.

## The three things that most made me trust them

1. **The engagement page (en-engagement-d-01 to -03).** Three clear models with time-to-first-result (2 / 2–6 / 6–12 weeks), a comparison table, a 5-step process with a named owner role per step, acceptance criteria and price agreed before building, and SaaS vs private (Docker) deployment. This is how a vendor who has done this before talks.
2. **Case pages structured like a delivery record (en-case-wiki-d-01, en-case-proposal-d-01).** System name, engagement type, a specific timeline ("4-week pilot · 8-week rollout"), and a before/after. Thin, but the skeleton is right.
3. **Honest disclaimers and process discipline.** "Anonymized; an engineer can demonstrate the full system under NDA", "illustrative data, not a commitment", "reply within one business day, assessment within a week". Restrained, not hype-y. The visual consistency also says someone cares about detail.

## What a strong vendor's site would have shown me that this one didn't

- **Named people:** real photos, names, LinkedIn links and prior employers for the leadership team.
- **At least one named, referenceable client,** or a logo strip, or "references available on request" with a named client exec quote. Even "Fortune 500 industrial manufacturer, 4,000 employees" with a role-attributed quote would help.
- **ROI in my language:** baseline volume, hours or FTE saved per month, cost per ticket or proposal, and payback period. Show the method behind 80% and 99%.
- **Security and compliance:** SOC 2 / ISO 27001 status, where data is hosted (US/Canada region option), which model providers are used, a "no training on your data" statement, and the legal entity and jurisdiction.
- **Commercials:** typical pilot price range, minimum engagement, and what managed service costs per month.
- **Fit for my industry:** a distribution or operations case (order status, quoting, inventory, supplier docs) and the integrations I use (M365/Teams, Salesforce, ServiceNow, NetSuite/SAP, EDI).
- **Company facts:** founded year, HQ address, headcount, offices.
- **Logistics:** English-language product screenshots on the English site, and a calendar link that actually books the 30-minute call.

## Would I book a call in 60 seconds?

**No.** I'd bookmark it as "interesting small shop, revisit if a pilot budget appears" and move on to a vendor whose people I can look up.

**The single change that would most increase the chance:** put real names, real photos and LinkedIn links on the four founders, on the home page and the About page, with their actual prior employers. Right now my first question is "who are these people and are they real?" Answering it in one glance turns every other claim on the site from "unverifiable" into "worth a 30-minute call". If you can do a second thing, add one named client reference or attributed quote.
