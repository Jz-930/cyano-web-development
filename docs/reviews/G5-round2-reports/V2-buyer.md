COO review of the Cyano English site (read-only, from screenshots in `F:\2026-11-10-Cyano Website Rebuild\docs\reviews\G5-round2-screens\`). I read every desktop page in the requested order plus `en-home-mobile-01` to `-08`; I skipped the last one or two bottom-aligned frames on a few pages (repeat CTA/footer).

## Five-second answer (from `en-home-d-01.jpg` only)
They build custom AI systems for companies: customer inquiries, proposal drafting, internal knowledge, data queries, plus managed AI workflows.
- **What they do:** about 80% confident. The sub-headline is concrete and did the work.
- **For whom:** about 30% confident. "Enterprise" is the only clue: no industry, no company size, no client logo.
- **Size and location:** no idea. The big glass "C" told me nothing; it could be a 5-person shop or a 500-person one.

## Scores (1–7)
1. **Maturity and reliability: 3.** The process thinking is mature (pilots, acceptance criteria, human sign-off). The company itself is invisible: no names, no faces, no address, no founding year, no headcount, no client names.
2. **Willingness to hand over an important project: 3.** I would consider a 2–6 week scoped pilot on a non-critical workflow. I would not give an anonymous four-founder team anything my operations depend on.
3. **Belief in real enterprise delivery: 3.** I believe they built these systems. I am not convinced that enterprise clients are running them at scale, because nothing is attributable.
4. **Overall professionalism: 5.** Consistent, well organized, sensible navigation, and the contact page says what happens next. A few visible defects cost it a point.
5. **First-impression polish: 5.** The hero and typography look like a capable team. Repeated stock-style renders and the bugs below pull it back toward "well-executed template".
6. **Effort to understand: 4.** Headlines and big numbers carry the story if I skim. Below that it is small monospaced labels and engineer vocabulary (ACL, RAG, DLP, "four-tier context budget") that I skipped.
7. **Are the two case studies real delivered projects? Unsure.**
   - **Proposal builder: leaning yes.** The Chinese-language production screen, the 10-week milestone breakdown and the "historical backtesting" phase read like real work. But "CYANO TECH" branding sits inside what is supposed to be a client system, and there is no client quote, user count or volume.
   - **IT wiki assistant: leaning no, or "demo dressed as a case".** The dashboard shows "Jane Doe", "Smart IT Bot" and suspiciously round numbers (80%, <3s, 1,200+, 15% = 10% + 5%). The "before" state has no numbers at all, and the sandbox admits it is preset prompts with no live data.
8. **Look and feel: premium at first glance, generic-AI on inspection.**
   - **Premium:** restrained type, dark/light rhythm, disciplined colour, good spacing.
   - **Generic/AI-generated:** glass and prism 3D renders everywhere, the same render reused four times on one page, a watercolor-style illustrated founding team, and decorative images where evidence should be.

## The three things that most made me trust them
1. **The engagement model page** (`en-engagement-d-02.jpg` to `-06.jpg`). It offers a 2–6 week pilot with "expand or stop", a side-by-side table of ownership and upkeep, and FAQs including "What happens if a pilot falls short?". That is how a vendor who has been burned by real projects talks.
2. **Human sign-off and honesty about limits.** Every workflow shows a human gate, a 15% escalation rate is shown rather than hidden, the copy promises to "advise honestly when a build is premature", and asterisks mark internal benchmarks.
3. **The proposal case study's delivery timeline** (`en-case-proposal-d-06.jpg`). Weeks 1–2 diagnosis, 3–6 build, 7–8 backtesting on past engagements, 9–10 rollout — specific enough to sound lived. The contact page's "what happens next" (`en-contact-d-02.jpg`) adds a reply in one business day and a written assessment in a week.

## What I expected on a strong vendor's site and did not find
- Real founder names, photos, LinkedIn links and named prior employers.
- At least one named client, a logo, or a quote attributed to a title and industry, with an offer of reference calls.
- Company facts: legal entity, office address, year founded, team size, phone number.
- Security and compliance evidence: SOC 2 / ISO 27001 status, data residency, which model providers see my data, a sample DPA.
- Business outcomes in my terms: hours or dollars saved, users, volumes, payback period, and whether the system is still in use a year later.
- A price anchor, even a range for a pilot.
- North American evidence: an English-language client screen, a US or Canadian client, and my stack (Microsoft 365, Salesforce, ServiceNow, an ERP).
- A calendar booking link and a named person I would be talking to.
- Something about my world. Distribution appears only as one "retail & supply chain" card I did not get to read in depth.

## Would I book a call with 60 seconds?
No, not today. I would bookmark it as "smart small team, probably China-based, unproven here" and move on to a vendor with a name I can check. If I had an acute proposal-drafting problem I might send the form for a pilot, but not for an important project.

**Single change that would most raise the odds:** replace the illustrated, nameless founders with real names, real photos and LinkedIn links, stating which Fortune Global 500 companies they worked at. It should sit on the home page, not only on About. The process and case content is already good enough to earn a call once I know real, checkable people stand behind it; a single attributed client quote would be the next most valuable addition.
